# Existing discovery benchmarks: source and temporal audit

Audited 11 October 2026. This is a primary-source metadata and methodology audit, not an executed benchmark or an item-level eligibility certificate. Evidence is recorded in [benchmark_sources.json](../../data/ground_truth/benchmark_sources.json). Only public landing pages, HTML papers, repository metadata, README files, and license files were read. No full dataset, paper PDF, or source-paper corpus was downloaded or redistributed.

**Decision: use all four resources as candidate sources or protocol references only. None is admitted here as a clean historical benchmark.** Benchmark publication dates concern the benchmark itself; the relevant historical boundary is the earliest disclosure of each exact target contribution. A dataset's stated cutoff or a prompt telling a model to act historically cannot establish that its weights exclude the target.

## What each resource can support

**ResearchBench** decomposes paper-derived evaluation into inspiration retrieval, hypothesis composition, and ranking across 12 disciplines. Its paper restricts targets to publications from 2024 onward. Use it to locate precursor relationships and naturalistic candidate tasks. This date rule does not establish item eligibility for a particular frozen checkpoint. Author-selected inspirations and extracted background supply assistance and can encode the target relationship. Rebuild inputs from historically eligible versions and record the selection assistance. [Paper record](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.644/), [official repository](https://github.com/ankitala/ResearchBench).

**DiscoveryBench** provides data-grounded tasks with datasets, metadata, and goals; its abstract reports 264 real tasks and 903 synthetic tasks. Use it for analysis workflows, candidate hypotheses, and verifier design. A success can depend on code execution, statistical analysis, and supplied goal framing. It is not automatically evidence of unaided conceptual invention or of an unknown historical result. The real tasks originate from published work, so their historical dates and input leakage require separate audits. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01725), [official repository](https://github.com/allenai/discoverybench).

**CUSP** concerns event-level scientific forecasting; current v2 reports 4,760 events and 17,429 questions. It derives temporal boundaries using the earliest observed date linked to a manuscript DOI across several services. Use those dates as search leads. DOI linkage can miss a differently titled preprint, an earlier version containing the target, code, or a talk. Its output auditor is more careful than the plan's summary implies. Under exempt outcomes it lists: “Correct predictions or methodologies that happen to match the paper.” Failure requires a verbatim post-cutoff named entity, verified timing, and a judgment that it could not have been independently invented. The quoted rule appears in v2 Appendix H.1 and v1 Appendix G.1, both *Evaluation System Prompts*. [Current paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22681v2), [v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22681v1).

**Reconstruction** gives models temporally restricted, anonymized bibliographies and evaluates recovery of a withheld paper idea. Current v3 explicitly separates prompt leakage from parametric contamination and adds date-versus-cutoff strata; v1 already acknowledges memorized seeds. The full bibliography remains selected by the later paper's authors and supplies hindsight guidance. Use the protocol as a reconstruction reference, with this assistance declared. Its LLM Match outcome is neither an independent correctness certificate nor a novelty certificate. The authors also identify unresolved human judge validation and matched candidate-count controls. [Current paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16645v3), [v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16645v1).

## Contamination and rediscovery decisions

**Judge the inputs and provenance before treating output similarity as evidence.** Independently recovering a later correct proposition is the intended positive outcome of historical rediscovery. Matching the later method or number is not itself contamination. A future paper title, distinctive system name, or citation can motivate investigation, but is not conclusive proof of a training example: the prompt, parser, retrieval tool, pre-disclosure terminology, or accidental name collision can explain it. Separate the anomalous output from its assessed origin, and retain UNKNOWN when origin cannot be established. CUSP's actual published auditor has explicit exemptions for matching methods and numbers, consistent with the [research programme's interpretation](../../Plan/research-plan.md). Its named-entity verdict still does not certify training history, and this audit did not execute its evaluator or compare prompt text with a pinned implementation.

**A reference set can be temporally valid and still be chosen with hindsight.** A later seed paper's bibliography or a curator's precursor pair can point directly toward the desired synthesis. Score such conditions as bibliography-conditioned or selected-prerequisite reconstruction. Report an unassisted condition and, where feasible, an independently selected historical corpus condition. Audit reference versions and excerpts, not just reference IDs: an old URL can serve a new revision, and an old abstract can be accompanied by a current page's later citation context. A frozen citation list is not a frozen corpus snapshot.

**Knowledge-cutoff strata are sensitivity analyses.** A documented pretraining cutoff omits possible exposure during post-training, distillation, retrieval, and model revisions; an inferred cutoff adds uncertainty. A later date is not proof of exclusion unless the actual immutable checkpoint and all served components predate disclosure. Date-before-cutoff is likewise not proof of membership or memorization. Cross-stratum performance differences mix different papers and difficulty and cannot isolate the causal effect of contamination. This is an audit judgment; Reconstruction v3 makes the narrower reachability/membership distinction explicitly.

For historical admission, require the exact target proposition, earlier equivalent-results search, earliest contribution-bearing disclosure, immutable model/tokenizer evidence including post-training, version-pinned permitted inputs, an independent derivation or executable verifier, and an assistance label. A checkpoint released strictly before disclosure offers stronger exclusion evidence than an advertised cutoff. It still does not establish novelty relative to overlooked precursors, autonomy, or scientific utility. An eligible rediscovery of an old result also does not establish novelty today.

## Outstanding limits and actions

No benchmark item was individually audited or admitted. No model, evaluator, judge calibration, corpus membership test, or scientific result was run. ResearchBench raw access remains gated; no terms were accepted. DiscoveryBench software permission, individual source-data terms, and Reconstruction release/terms remain unresolved. Paper histories give exact arXiv timestamps but no universal first-disclosure proof; ACL's ResearchBench month cannot be upgraded to a day. The browser failed to render ResearchBench's DATA_LICENSE page, so the official raw file was successfully read instead. Some browser HTML searches omitted the CUSP auditor block; direct public HTML inspection supplied it. These access limitations and fallbacks are recorded in the manifest.

Proceed with newly authored controlled worlds for the composition assay. For later historical work, select a small number of candidates from these resources, independently reconstruct their target and chronology, and admit only those passing the item and checkpoint audit. Preserve ambiguous cases as exploratory rather than silently counting them as temporally clean successes.
