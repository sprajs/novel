# Independent audit of historical target dates and antecedents

Audited 11 October 2026 against primary paper versions, publisher sites, and public repository metadata. This audit follows the stronger historical admission rule in [the research plan](../../Plan/research-plan.md): release the actual checkpoint, post-training, adapters, and prompting interface before the earliest public target contribution. The [model audit](model_audit.md) and [machine-readable evidence](../../data/ground_truth/model_audit.json) give the frozen revisions. No weights, inference results, or clean scientific-discovery items are produced here.

**Actionable result:** retain the narrow DPO algebra lead with April 2023 Pythia as a conditional candidate. Reject generic memory-efficient attention as a FlashAttention-specific target. Reject generic low-rank adaptation, additive adapters, or merging as a LoRA-specific target. April 2025 Qwen snapshots support a separate conditional timing comparison for MeanFlow and AlphaEvolve; the July tokenizer interface is a different condition. An incomplete first-public-channel search keeps every lead exploratory.

**Dates are different kinds of evidence.** “Known paper date” below means the arXiv submission record for the inspected v1. The record dates a submission, not an independently archived public announcement hour. A Git commit dates retained repository content, not its first public push or a private-to-public switch. A publisher release statement corroborates release without automatically proving an exact weight SHA was publicly accessible then. “Earliest verified” means earliest inspected attributable channel; it never means all earlier channels have been excluded.

**FlashAttention has at least three different possible targets.** The online-softmax recurrence is already in [Milakov and Gimelshein v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02867v1), with sequential running maximum/normalizer and an associative merge in Sections 3–3.1 of the [original paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.02867v1). Merely regenerating that recurrence is a reconstruction of an older result. Its [official code repository](https://github.com/NVIDIA/online-softmax) has creation metadata dated July 26, 2018 and an initial retained commit dated July 27; the [API](https://api.github.com/repos/NVIDIA/online-softmax/commits?per_page=1) does not establish public visibility at either instant. The May v1 already discloses the method.

[Rabe and Staats v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05682v1) goes further: exact attention can accumulate a scaled value numerator and normalizer instead of retaining the full attention matrix. Sections 2–4 of the [versioned original text](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05682v1) provide the stable update and a chunked accelerator implementation; Section 5.2 treats differentiation. Its memory-complexity convention excludes output storage. The external Google Research folder has a [retained January 25, 2022 commit](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/commit/db5c5673373434ce3a1264fa3134c8ba5559542d), according to the [path history API](https://api.github.com/repos/google-research/google-research/commits?path=memory_efficient_attention&per_page=100); executable implementation text is already in the December paper. The repository's later code date cannot postpone disclosure of the algorithm.

[FlashAttention v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135v1) targets an IO-aware tiled GPU schedule and an HBM/SRAM complexity analysis, with recomputation and a fused implementation. The [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.14135v1), Section 3 and Appendix B, distinguishes those contributions from a streaming exact-attention identity; it cites Rabe–Staats. Its PDF title page says May 30 while the arXiv record gives May 27. Use the version record rather than the PDF's internal date. The paper also acknowledges NVIDIA Apex FMHA code as an implementation starting point; claiming novelty for arbitrary fused attention would require a further kernel-history comparison.

There is an additional earlier channel to resolve: the [official first retained commit](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention/commit/1fcbe6f0d088d807ba585ddb850eb3497cd7b65b), dated May 20, 2022, contains an [alpha-release README](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention/blob/1fcbe6f0d088d807ba585ddb850eb3497cd7b65b/README.md), attention interfaces, and CUDA forward/backward files. [Repository metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/Dao-AILab/flash-attention) dates repository creation to May 19, 2022. This proves that an earlier implementation survives in the history today; it does not prove the repository was public on May 20. Consequently, May 27 cannot be treated as a fully cleared first-disclosure boundary.

The [HAET 2022 schedule](https://haet2022.github.io/schedule) dates the ICML workshop to July 23; its [accepted-paper list](https://haet2022.github.io/accepter_papers) includes FlashAttention. The [Stanford MLSys schedule](https://mlsys.stanford.edu/) lists a January 11, 2023 talk. These are inspected later public channels and do not establish absence of earlier talks, abstracts, slides, or theses. No independently dated pre-May-27 public talk was verified in this audit.

The next gate for a FlashAttention lead is to specify the exact tiled schedule or IO statement the verifier will require, compare it with Rabe–Staats and earlier kernels, and establish a checkpoint's release before the unresolved earlier-code boundary. None of the audited Pythia or Qwen releases is old enough. A prompt that already supplies the tiled IO strategy must be labeled method assistance; solving its bookkeeping is not unassisted discovery of that strategy.

**LoRA also needs a narrower contribution than its name.** [LoRA v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685v1) specifies a frozen dense weight plus trainable rank-factorized update in [Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.09685v1). Its motivating prerequisite is [Aghajanyan, Zettlemoyer, and Gupta](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255), submitted December 22, 2020. [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08838), submitted April 24, 2018, and [Houlsby et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751v1), submitted February 2, 2019, provide earlier low-dimensional optimization and NLP-adapter context. These are prerequisites and related methods, not proof that every detail of LoRA was already available.

The stronger conflict is [Zhao, Li, and Gong's 2016 low-rank plus diagonal adaptation](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/low-rank-plus-diagonal-adaptation-for-deep-neural-networks/). In the [publisher-hosted original paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/ICASSP2016_LRPD-1.pdf), Sections 3.2–3.3 parameterize speaker adaptation as a diagonal component plus two low-rank factors, including an identity-plus-low-rank case, and train the parameters by backpropagation. This is an antecedent to a broad low-rank-adaptation proposition. Its transformations, placement, and speech setting differ from LoRA; exact algebraic and architectural equivalence is not established here. The publication year is verified, while an earliest public PDF/code/talk day is unresolved.

An even closer additive/mergeability precedent is [Rebuffi, Bilen, and Vedaldi 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10082v1). Section 3.1.2 of the [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.10082v1) places a residual adapter in parallel with a fixed convolutional filter, expresses the resulting filter as an additive adjustment, and permits fusion at test time. Section 3.4 factorizes the adapter into low-rank matrices and fine-tunes the factors after compression. These facts defeat a generic target of “add a low-rank parallel update and merge it.” A remaining LoRA-specific target could involve its Transformer attention placement, initialization/training prescription, or a rank-related finding, but those must be stated and compared individually. Reproducing a factorization identity cannot reproduce the empirical rank evidence.

The [Microsoft LoRA repository API](https://api.github.com/repos/microsoft/LoRA) dates creation to June 18, 2021. Its [oldest retained main commit](https://github.com/microsoft/LoRA/commit/2eb74c2dc816ffb89a8217158a837952df7cbcfe) is dated September 18, 2021. The gap illustrates why current history cannot supply an earliest-code-release day by itself. June 17 v1 is the known paper channel; no earlier public author talk, slide, thesis, technical report, patent, or equivalent algorithm was ruled out comprehensively. There is no audited pre-LoRA model in this artifact.

**DPO is still a conditional algebra lead.** The [May 29 v1 record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290v1) and [v1 Section 4/Appendix A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18290v1) establish the paper's specific reward-policy substitution and cancellation of the context-only partition term under pairwise preference likelihood. April 3 Pythia release evidence precedes that known channel. This supports proceeding with symbolic/finite-action checking elsewhere in the project; it does not certify first mathematical disclosure or empirical alignment results.

Broader “preference optimization without PPO” claims have earlier nearby sources: [RRHF v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05302v1), April 11, 2023, and [SLiC-HF v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10425v1), May 17, 2023. Exact equivalence to the chosen DPO algebra is not asserted here. The [official code repository](https://github.com/eric-mitchell/direct-preference-optimization) has a [June 22 creation record](https://api.github.com/repos/eric-mitchell/direct-preference-optimization) and [June 22 initial retained commit](https://github.com/eric-mitchell/direct-preference-optimization/commit/e05cf705c1d62719a591a647513e8a0acb3b332d), later than the preprint. This does not exclude another earlier code repository or author presentation. Selecting exactly the two helpful equations is hindsight assistance; a bridge that says to cancel the normalizer supplies the target operation and must be reported as such.

**May 2025 is a separate temporal cohort.** The [Qwen3 April 29 publisher release](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/) corroborates complete April snapshots. It precedes the [May 14 AlphaEvolve announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) and [MeanFlow's May 19 v1 record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447v1). The original and modern Qwen chat templates differ. The model audit supplies complete April revisions and byte identities; precise targets and earlier public channels remain to be resolved by the broader registry audit. The date comparison alone does not admit either lead.

**Admission work remaining:** obtain historical public-visibility corroboration for exact model revisions; name a precise target proposition and its best earlier equivalent; date author talks/slides/theses/code/abstracts rather than treating a search miss as absence; audit every premise, bridge, adapter, and retrieval input; then verify prerequisite competence and output correctness. Keep unresolved cases exploratory with the specific missing evidence recorded. This audit found concrete conflicts and usable frozen candidates, and found no basis to label a historical item clean.
